01 — Academics

Academic Support

Reliable places to learn, revise and catch up when a lecture did not make sense the first time.

NPTEL

Free engineering and science courses recorded by IIT and IISc faculty. Useful for strengthening core subjects and for certification.

Visit NPTEL →

SWAYAM

A government platform with school, undergraduate and postgraduate courses from Indian universities, many of them free to audit.

Visit SWAYAM →

MIT OpenCourseWare

Lecture notes, assignments and exams from MIT courses. Helpful when you want a second explanation of a difficult topic.

Visit MIT OCW →

Khan Academy

Short, beginner-friendly explanations of maths, physics and computing basics — a good place to rebuild fundamentals.

Visit Khan Academy →

Study tip: pick one platform for a subject and finish it, instead of collecting links from five different sites.

02 — Community

Clubs & Communities

College is more than lectures. Clubs are where you build a team, learn to present, and meet seniors who can guide you.

Technical clubs

Coding, robotics, AI/ML or electronics clubs. They usually run workshops and help teams prepare for hackathons.

Ask your department office for the current club list.

Cultural & literary clubs

Debate, drama, music, dance and media clubs. Great for confidence, communication and campus events.

Most clubs recruit during the first weeks of a semester.

Open-source communities

Communities like GitHub and freeCodeCamp let you contribute to real projects and learn from developers outside your college.

Explore GitHub →

Hackathon teams

Hackathons are usually better with a mixed team — one designer, one backend developer, one presenter. Start looking early.

Browse Devpost →

03 — Career

Career Growth

Start small and early: a clear resume, one good project and a few real applications are worth more than a long list of certificates.

Internships

Internshala lists internships and live training programmes for students across India, with filters for work-from-home roles.

Visit Internshala →

Practice coding

Solve a few problems every week to keep your fundamentals sharp and to prepare for technical interview rounds.

Visit HackerRank →

Build a portfolio

Host your projects on GitHub and publish them with GitHub Pages. A live link is far more convincing than a screenshot.

Learn GitHub Pages →

Learn a skill properly

freeCodeCamp offers free, project-based tracks in web development and programming, with certificates at the end.

Visit freeCodeCamp →

04 — Wellbeing

Student Wellbeing

Marks are important, but they are not the whole picture. Sleep, food, friends and asking for help are part of doing well.

Talk to someone

Your college counsellor or a trusted teacher is usually the fastest support available. You do not need a "big enough" reason to ask.

Check your college notice board for counselling hours.

Helplines in India

Tele-MANAS is a free national mental health helpline available 24×7, in multiple languages.

Tele-MANAS: 14416 →

Manage exam stress

WHO publishes short, practical guides on stress, sleep and staying healthy during demanding study periods.

Visit WHO →

Balance and rest

Plan your week around fixed sleep and one non-academic activity. A tired brain studies slower, not longer.

Small, repeatable habits beat last-night cramming.

CampusMate is an informational page, not a medical or counselling service. In an emergency, contact local emergency services or your college authorities immediately.

05 — Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Click a question to open the answer.

What is CampusMate?

CampusMate is a single page that collects useful resources for college students — academic material, clubs, career links and wellbeing support — so you do not have to search many sites at once.

Is CampusMate free to use?

Yes. The website itself is free, and every resource linked from it is a publicly available website.

Do I need to create an account?

No. There is no login, no sign-up and no data collection. You just open the page and use the links.

Which technologies were used to build it?

Only HTML5 for the structure and CSS3 for the design and responsive layout. No JavaScript, no frameworks and no build tools.

Are the resources official college resources?

No. The links point to well-known public learning platforms. Your own college website, library and notice boards remain the official source for course and club information.

Will more features be added later?

The plan is to replace the generic links with verified college-specific resources, and later add a search feature once JavaScript and a backend are part of the project.
